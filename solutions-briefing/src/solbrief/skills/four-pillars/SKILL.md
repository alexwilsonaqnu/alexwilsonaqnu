# The four pillars of a solution card

Solutions journalism (as defined by the Solutions Journalism Network) covers *responses* to problems,
rigorously. A card is only useful to a reporter if it documents all four pillars, each backed by a quotation
from a source the reporter can open.

## 1. Response — what is being done, by whom, where, since when
- One plain sentence: the intervention, the organizations running it, the place(s), and the start date if known.
- Name real organizations and places exactly as the source does. No invented programs, cities, or dates.
- Supported by at least one verbatim quote (pillar `response`).

## 2. Insight — why it works, and what transfers
- **Mechanism:** the causal idea in one or two sentences ("housing comes first; treatment is offered, not required").
- **Transferability:** what another place would need for it to work there, and where evidence is thin
  (e.g. "studies were urban; no rural evaluations").
- Supported by at least one verbatim quote (pillar `insight`).

## 3. Evidence — does it work?
- Each claim is one sentence, with exactly one source URL and one verbatim supporting span from that page.
- A claim may not contain any number, percentage, or date that is not in its span.
- Do not upgrade the strength of a finding: "may", "potentially positive", "mixed", "no discernible effect"
  must be carried through.
- Prefer the strongest independent sources: systematic reviews, randomized trials, quasi-experimental
  evaluations, then administrative data. Aim for at least **two independent publishers** (a card with one
  publisher stays *provisional* and is always labelled as such).

## 4. Limitations — what to probe
- Cost, context-dependence, populations or places not studied, failures elsewhere, open questions.
- Each limitation is one sentence with a source URL and a verbatim supporting span.
- A card with no sourced limitation does not publish.

## Item types — never blur them
- **Solution lead:** a documented response to a systemic problem (all four pillars).
- **Service guidance:** immediate, protective how-to tied to an acute event (shelter in place, boil-water
  notices). Shorter; sourced only to authoritative agencies (NWS, CDC, FEMA, state health departments).

## Experts
- Only people named on a public institutional page (faculty page, agency staff page, media-relations office).
- `contact_path_url` must be that https page. Never personal email addresses or phone numbers.
- Stance: `researcher` (evaluated it), `practitioner` (runs it), `critic` (has published skepticism).
